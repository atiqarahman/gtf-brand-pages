The Problem

The Discovery Gap Your Customers Face

A woman in Dubai is planning what to wear to a destination wedding in Udaipur. She wants something that honours Indian craft without looking like every other lehenga at the sangeet. She's scrolling Instagram and sees one of your Khurkhul silk pieces — the drape, the luminosity, the way the Meitei motifs sit against a modern silhouette. She wants it. She searches online. She gets Sabyasachi knockoffs and generic "Indian designer lehenga." She can't find you. That sale — and a customer who would have become a lifelong collector — is lost.

11 Tareng is something the fashion world barely knows exists yet: a contemporary couture label born in Imphal, Manipur, founded in 2023 by Jayshree Koijam and Reena Ahanthem — two fashion professionals who decided that Manipur's living textile traditions deserve more than museum preservation. They deserve a global stage. At the heart of 11 Tareng is handwoven Khurkhul silk, traditionally associated with the dhoti and phanek, reimagined into corsetry, tailored jackets, fluid separates, and sculptural lehengas. Meitei motifs and intricate embroidery are woven into every design — not as decoration, but as cultural identity meeting contemporary precision.

Instagram builds your audience. It doesn't convert globally at scale. And traditional marketplaces can't communicate why a Khurkhul silk jacket from Imphal is different from a generic "embroidered Indian jacket" — they strip away the heritage, the craft, the story that makes the garment irreplaceable.
