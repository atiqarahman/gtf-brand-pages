The Problem

Fashion Search Is Fundamentally Broken.

Search "sexy but not too revealing party dress" on Farfetch. The first result is a Pinko t-shirt with a bow print. 5,700+ results — not a single one that understood what you were actually looking for.

This isn't a Farfetch problem. It's an industry-wide infrastructure failure. Every fashion marketplace on earth — from Amazon to Net-a-Porter to Myntra — forces customers to translate their feelings into hard attributes: neckline, sleeve length, hemline, colour. Categories. Filters. Checkboxes.

But that's not how people think about fashion.

How People Actually Search

"Hailey Bieber chic sophisticated vibe"
— Style reference + emotional descriptor. No filter can process this.
"Outfits for Namu's in Dubai"
— Location-specific occasion. No marketplace indexes venues as fashion attributes.
"Something that makes my ex regret everything"
— Pure emotion. A great stylist knows exactly what this means. A search bar returns nothing.
"Bandra by the beach outfit — cute but not trying too hard"
— Destination + vibe + social intent. This is a Pinterest search. It should be a shopping search.
"Victoria Beckham vibes but sexy, under $400"
— Style reference + modifier + budget. The customer knows exactly what she wants. No platform can find it.

These aren't invented for a pitch deck. These are real queries from real customers. And they represent a fundamental shift: in a world where people talk to ChatGPT and Claude in natural language, where Google is rebuilding its entire search product around AI because traditional search is declining — fashion commerce hasn't caught up.

The gap isn't discovery. It's translation. The customer knows what she wants — she just can't express it in filter language. And the platform can't understand her in human language. That translation layer doesn't exist. We're building it.

The Insight

Social Media Created the Demand. Nobody Built the Commerce Layer.

Pinterest and Instagram fundamentally changed how people think about fashion. They taught an entire generation to associate clothing with vibes, feelings, locations, and cultural moments — not categories and SKUs.

A woman scrolling Instagram now thinks in terms of "cool girl energy," "quiet luxury," "that Bombay beach vibe," "Dubai brunch aesthetic." She saves mood boards. She screenshots outfits. She knows exactly what she wants to feel.

But when she goes to actually buy? She's back to: Category → Dresses → Neckline → Sleeve Length → Color → Price. The emotional language she used to discover what she wanted is completely useless on the platform where she's supposed to buy it.

How Every Platform Works Today

Customer has emotional intent → Platform forces clinical input → Customer decodes her own feelings into hard attributes → Decision fatigue → Defaults to same high-street brands → Complains about quality → Or overpays for designer she already knows

How GTF Works

Customer has emotional intent → AI understands the feeling → Translates it into 14+ product attributes (vibe, occasion, silhouette, material, style reference, destination) → Surfaces relevant products → Builds trust via UGC + virtual try-on → Converts

This is not "search with AI features." This is a fundamentally different commerce architecture. The same shift that's happening in every industry — from tools to end-to-end AI products — is coming to fashion. We're building the end-to-end product, not just a tool.

The Vibe Framework — Our Proprietary Data Layer

Every product on GTF is indexed across two layers:

Hard Attributes (10)

Category, colour, silhouette, length, neckline, sleeve, pattern, price tier, material. Single-value, controlled vocabulary. Standard — every marketplace has these.

Soft Attributes (4) — Our Moat

Vibe/Aesthetic, Occasion, Style References, Destination Vibes. Multi-value arrays (3-5 values each). This is the emotional layer. No other marketplace has it. It's what lets us understand "Hailey Bieber chic" or "Dubai brunch energy."

The soft attribute layer is what makes GTF fundamentally different from adding AI search to Shopify. It's a proprietary dataset that compounds with every product indexed, every query processed, every purchase made. Over time, we build the most comprehensive map of how people emotionally relate to fashion — not just what they buy, but why.

The Product

End-to-End AI Commerce. Not a Tool.

The industry trend in AI is clear: the value is moving from standalone tools to end-to-end products that own the full workflow. GTF is an end-to-end AI fashion commerce product — from intent understanding through to purchase and post-purchase intelligence.

The defensibility isn't the AI model — it's the data. Every query teaches us how people emotionally relate to fashion. Every product we index adds to the vibe graph. Every purchase validates or corrects the matching. This flywheel compounds, and no competitor can replicate it without the same volume of emotional fashion data.

Why Now + Company

The Timing Is Structural, Not Cyclical.

  • Consumer search behavior has permanently shifted. Google search volume is declining. People are habituated to natural language from ChatGPT/Claude. Fashion search needs to catch up or die. The platforms that figure out emotional-intent commerce win the next decade.
  • AI has moved from tools to end-to-end products. The market is rewarding complete AI products, not bolt-on features. GTF is built AI-native from day one — not an existing marketplace with a chatbot added on top.
  • Social media created demand it can't fulfil. Instagram and Pinterest taught a generation to discover fashion by vibe. But they're not commerce platforms. The customer who finds inspiration on Instagram has nowhere to convert that inspiration into a purchase using the same emotional language.
  • $300 AOV = premium unit economics. This isn't a $30 fast-fashion marketplace. At 35-40% commission on $300 orders, we generate $105-$120 gross revenue per transaction. The math works from day one, without scale.
